The visa application starts on the official pre-application system visa.gov.tr, which redirects you to the e-Visa if you meet its conditions, or to an appointment at the consulate.

Visa regulations change on the principle of reciprocity, and the rules for diplomatic and special passports and truck drivers may differ. Check the official source before buying your ticket, or send us your nationality and we will confirm the rule in force.

General rules

Rules that apply to all travelers to Turkey

Passport validity

Your passport must remain valid for at least 60 days beyond your permitted length of stay. For example, someone entering with a 90-day exemption needs a passport valid for at least 150 days on the day of entry. The Turkish Ministry of Foreign Affairs advises 6 months of validity.

90 days within 180 days

The length of stay on a visa or exemption does not exceed 90 days in every 180 days, and the rule applies to all foreigners. Leaving and coming back does not start a new count, and holding two passports does not double the period.

The rule follows the passport

What determines whether you need a visa is the nationality of your passport, not your country of residence. And what makes the e-Visa available to some nationalities is specifically a valid visa or residence permit from Schengen countries, the US, the UK or Ireland.

Travel documents

Holders of travel documents (other than passports) need a visa from Turkish missions before travel, whatever the rule of the country that issued the document.

Tourism and business only

The e-Visa is valid for tourism and business only. Work, study and long-term residence require visas issued by Turkish embassies and consulates.

Before you pay

A visa does not guarantee entry, as the final decision rests with the border authorities. Visa fees are not refunded if you are refused, and health insurance covering the length of your stay is required. The Turkish Ministry of Foreign Affairs advises applying at least a month before travel.

Questions & answers

Turkey visa FAQ

Does a Saudi citizen need a visa to enter Turkey?

No. Holders of a regular Saudi passport are exempt from the visa for up to 90 days in any 180 days, as are citizens of the UAE, Bahrain and Oman. The passport must be valid for at least 150 days on the day of entry.

Does an Egyptian citizen need a visa to enter Turkey?

Yes. Holders of a regular Egyptian passport need a visa. Those under 15 or over 45 get the e-Visa with no additional condition, and those between 15 and 45 get it if they hold a valid visa or residence permit from Schengen, the US, the UK or Ireland and travel on specific airlines. Otherwise, the application is made through the consulate.

I live in Saudi Arabia or the UAE and my nationality is not exempt. Does residence exempt me?

No. The rule follows the nationality of the passport, not the country of residence. The documents that allow the e-Visa for some nationalities are a valid visa or residence permit from Schengen countries, the US, the UK or Ireland. Send us your nationality and age and we will work out the route available to you.

How long must my passport be valid to enter Turkey?

At least 60 days beyond your permitted length of stay. Someone entering with an exemption or a 90-day visa needs 150 days, and someone entering with a 30-day visa needs 90 days. The safest is 6 months of validity, as the Turkish Ministry of Foreign Affairs advises.

Can I stay 90 days, then leave and come straight back?

No. The maximum is 90 days in every 180 days, and all your days in Turkey during the last 180 days are counted. Those who want to stay longer apply for a short-term residence permit from the Immigration Administration in Turkey.

What is the difference between the e-Visa and the consulate visa?

The e-Visa is requested online at evisa.gov.tr for eligible nationalities, and is for tourism and business only. The consulate (sticker) visa starts with an application on the visa.gov.tr system and then an appointment at the Turkish consulate, and is for those who do not meet the e-Visa conditions or for other purposes such as work and study.
