Destination guide • Trabzon

Tourism in Trabzon and Black Sea tours

Tourism in Trabzon (also spelled Trebizond) means mist over green forests, open plateaus, mountain lakes, and a glass of tea on a balcony overlooking the Black Sea. This is your guide to its best places and the best way to plan your trip.

Why Trabzon?

Trabzon at a glance

Trabzon is a coastal city in northeastern Turkey on the Black Sea, and the main gateway to what Gulf visitors call "Turkey's north." It is surrounded by forested mountains that get rain for most of the year, so it stays green even in midsummer, and its weather remains mild in July and August when temperatures climb in the Gulf. That is why it sees a growing number of visitors, with direct flights from several Gulf cities in the summer season, on schedules that change every year.

Trabzon's real strength is its surroundings: Lake Uzungöl, about 100 km away, Ayder Plateau in Rize, Sümela Monastery hanging on a cliff face inside Altındere National Park, and Haydarnabi Plateau and Lake Sera, both close to the city. That is why itineraries are usually built around day trips by car from your hotel, or around moving between several overnight stops. On the way to these places you pass terraced tea plantations, shops selling honey and knives, and wooden villages scattered over the slopes.

In the city itself you will find the historic Hagia Sophia Mosque overlooking the sea, Atatürk Palace among the pine trees, Boztepe Hill with its cafés and wide views, and Meydan Square and Uzun Sokak for shopping. Don't miss Akçaabat köfte, hamsi (anchovy) fish and the muhlama dish of melted cheese, with a glass of the region's famous tea. The city also has modern shopping centers and local markets for regional products, which makes it a comfortable base for longer stays.

The mountain roads in the north are winding and the distances between plateaus are fairly long, so most visitors prefer a private car with an English-speaking driver who knows the roads and the changeable weather. We arrange this as part of the 7-day Trabzon and Uzungöl itinerary or the 10-day Black Sea tour, with hotels, villas or wooden cabins as you prefer. If you are coming from Istanbul, you can combine the two cities in the 10-day Istanbul and Trabzon itinerary, with a domestic flight between them.

Top sights

The best places to visit in Trabzon

A carefully chosen list of what is worth your time in Trabzon, with smart visit planning inside our itineraries.

Sümela Monastery

A historic monastery carved into a towering cliff above the Altındere Valley, about 45 km south of the city. Reaching it takes a short uphill walk, and the view of the valley alone is worth the trip.

Hagia Sophia Mosque in Trabzon

Built in the 13th century, it now stands as a mosque on a green hill overlooking the Black Sea, surrounded by a quiet garden that is perfect for a rest.

Atatürk Palace

An elegant white palace among the pine trees on the city's heights, dating from the late Ottoman period. Atatürk stayed here during his visit to the city, and its gardens are a lovely place for photos.

Boztepe Hill

The highest viewpoint over the city and the port, with cafés and traditional tea gardens. The best time to visit is just before sunset.

Meydan Square and Uzun Sokak (Long Street)

Trabzon's commercial heart: clothing shops, sweets, nuts, honey and casual restaurants, and a pleasant area for an evening walk.

Lake Sera

A quiet lake west of the city, about 15 km away, surrounded by trees, cafés and restaurants, and ideal for a short family outing.

Lake Uzungöl

One of the best-known places in Turkey's Black Sea region: a mountain lake surrounded by forests and wooden cabins, with the famous mosque on its shore. Details in the Uzungöl tourism guide.

Ayder Plateau

A plateau in Rize province at an altitude of about 1,300 meters, with the Gelin Tülü waterfall, wooden houses and hot springs, about 170 km from Trabzon.

Haydarnabi Plateau

A green plateau about an hour from the city, known for views above the clouds in the early morning and rustic grill restaurants.

Zigana Pass and Hamsiköy village

A mountain road through the forests that passes Hamsiköy, famous for sütlaç (rice pudding) cooked with the region's milk, and is often combined with a visit to Sümela Monastery.

Çal Cave and Karaca Cave

Two caves with striking limestone formations: Çal, close to the city in the Düzköy area, and Karaca, in Gümüşhane province beyond the Zigana Pass. Good for children and for rainy days.

Tea gardens in Rize and the Fırtına Valley

Rize is the capital of Turkish tea, and its terraced plantations on the slopes are an unforgettable sight. The Fırtına Valley has old stone bridges and river rafting in summer.

Day Trips

Tours and day trips from Trabzon

  • Uzungöl trip: About two hours by car along the coast and then up the valley, with stops at tea factories, knife and honey shops and a waterfall on the way, then free time around the lake.
  • Ayder and Rize: A long day trip to Ayder Plateau and its waterfall, stopping at the tea gardens in Rize and the Fırtına Valley with its stone bridges.
  • Sümela, Zigana and Hamsiköy: A visit to Sümela Monastery inside Altındere National Park, then the mountain road over Zigana and a taste of rice pudding in Hamsiköy.
  • Haydarnabi, Lake Sera and Çal Cave: A day close to the city that combines a plateau, a lake and a cave, suitable for your first day or for families with young children.
  • Trabzon city tour: Hagia Sophia, Atatürk Palace and Boztepe Hill, then shopping in Meydan and Uzun Sokak and the modern shopping centers.
  • Batumi in Georgia (optional): Via the Sarp border crossing, about 200 km from Trabzon. First check Georgia's entry requirements for your nationality; we help you with this before booking.

Where to stay?

The best areas to stay in Trabzon

  • City center (Meydan): Best if you want restaurants, shopping and services next door, and prefer day trips to changing hotels. There are many hotels of different kinds here.
  • Yomra and Akçaabat on the coast: Apartments and hotels with sea views near the airport, quieter than the city center, and good for families and longer stays.
  • Uzungöl: For waking up to the view of the lake and mountains; one or two nights are usually enough. Places fill up quickly in July and August.
  • Ayder: Wooden cabins and a charming mountain atmosphere, suited to couples and small families. We advise large families to choose another option, because rooms there are often small.
  • Villas and country houses: Villas with a pool or garden in the surrounding countryside, ideal for large families who want privacy, with a car and driver needed to get around.

OYA tips

Tips before traveling to Trabzon

  • The weather in the north is changeable and rain is possible even in summer, so bring a light jacket and an umbrella, and leave one flexible day in your itinerary so you can swap tours around the fog.
  • Distances are long and the mountain roads wind: Uzungöl is about two hours away and Ayder about three. Don't plan more than one distant destination in a single day, and bring motion-sickness remedies for children.
  • A private car with an English-speaking driver is the practical choice here, because public transport to the plateaus is limited, and the driver knows the best times to visit.
  • July and August are the peak of the Gulf season, so book villas and cabins early. May, June and September are quieter, and nature is beautiful then.
  • Food in the region is halal, and the area is known for fish, köfte, corn dishes, cheese and mountain honey. Buy honey from trusted shops, because quality and prices vary a lot.
  • Mobile coverage is sometimes weak on the plateaus, so get a local SIM card at the airport and download maps in advance.

Questions & answers

Frequently asked questions about visiting Trabzon

How far is Trabzon from Istanbul?

About 1,070 km by road, around 13–14 hours by car, so most travelers prefer a domestic flight of about two hours from Istanbul Airport or Sabiha Gökçen to Trabzon Airport (TZX).

What is the best time to visit Trabzon?

From May to September, when the plateaus are green and the roads to them are open. July and August are the peak. Winter is beautiful for snow lovers, but some mountain roads and high plateaus may close temporarily.

How many days are enough to visit Trabzon and its surroundings?

5 days are enough for the city, Uzungöl and Sümela. 7 days add Ayder and Haydarnabi at a relaxed pace, and 10 days allow a full tour of the Black Sea region. See our 7-day Trabzon and Uzungöl itinerary.

Where should I stay: in Trabzon city or in Uzungöl?

It is best to combine both: one or two nights in Uzungöl to enjoy the lake in the morning and evening, and the remaining nights in Trabzon, where you have services, restaurants and easy access to the other tours.

Is Trabzon expensive compared with Istanbul?

Food and transport are mostly cheaper than in central Istanbul, but prices for villas and cabins rise in July and August at peak demand. Booking early, or traveling in May, June and September, gives you better options.

Do I need a private driver in Trabzon?

We strongly recommend one. The plateaus and lakes are far apart and there is no convenient public transport to them. We provide a private car with an English-speaking driver for the whole itinerary.

Can I visit Trabzon in winter?

Yes. The coastal city is relatively mild, while Uzungöl and the plateaus are covered in snow. Some high roads do close at times, so we choose tours day by day according to the weather.
