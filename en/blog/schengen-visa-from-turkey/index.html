Schengen visa from Turkey step by step: preparing your file, the appointment and tips to avoid refusal

Everything a Turkish citizen or foreign resident of Turkey needs to submit a strong Schengen file: where to apply, which documents, and which mistakes lead to rejection.

Applications for a Schengen visa from Turkey rise every year, both from Turkish citizens and from Arabs legally resident in Istanbul and elsewhere. At the same time, getting appointments in busy seasons is getting harder. In this guide we explain who is entitled to apply from Turkey, where to apply, what documents are required and how to avoid common mistakes.

Note: The information here is based on the latest announcements from the European Commission and consulates at the time of writing, and requirements may differ from one consulate to another and change without notice. The decision to grant a visa belongs to the consulate alone.

Who can apply for a Schengen visa from Turkey?

  • Turkish citizens residing in Turkey.
  • Foreigners legally resident in Turkey, including Arabs holding a residence card (the Turkish residence permit). Under the European visa code, the applicant applies at the consulate that covers their place of legal residence.

Many consulates require the residence permit to be valid for a period beyond the return date, often at least three months, but this varies from one consulate to another. Holders of a temporary protection card usually face practical difficulties in applying, and we assess each case individually before starting.

Which consulate should you apply to?

The general rule is to apply at the country that is your main destination, meaning the one where you will spend the longest time. If the stays are equal, you apply at the country of first entry. We advise you not to choose a country based on rumors about the “easiest country to give Schengen,” because an illogical file may be refused at any consulate, and a refusal stays on your record.

Schengen visa documents from Turkey

The list differs slightly between consulates, but the usual file includes:

  1. A signed application form and two recent biometric photos.
  2. A passport issued within the last ten years and valid for at least three months after departure, with two blank pages, along with copies of previous visas.
  3. Medical travel insurance with coverage of no less than 30,000 euros that covers the whole trip.
  4. A round-trip flight reservation, and a hotel reservation or an invitation letter.
  5. A bank statement for the last three months, stamped by the bank.
  6. For employees: a letter from the employer specifying the leave, payslips, and the social security record (SGK).
  7. For business owners: the tax certificate, the trade registry and an activity document.
  8. For students: a student certificate, with the sponsor's financial documents.
  9. For non-Turks: the Turkish residence permit card with a copy of it.

We help you prepare the right flight reservation and hotel reservation for your file, checking that they are consistent with your travel plan.

If your children are traveling with you

Each child needs a separate file that includes the birth certificate, the passport and copies of the parents' passports. If the child will travel with only one parent, a notarized consent from the parent who is not traveling is usually required. And don't forget that fees for children are lower than for adults, and that children under six are exempt from them as of the latest announced update.

The tiered visa rule for Turkish citizens

In July 2025 the European Union adopted more flexible rules for Turkish citizens residing in Turkey: after obtaining two visas and using them lawfully during the previous three years, a multiple-entry visa can be granted for one year, then for three years, then for five years. But these steps are not automatic, and they depend on proper use of previous visas and the validity of the passport.

The European Entry/Exit System and ETIAS

  • The Entry/Exit System (EES): It has been fully operational since April 2026 and has replaced passport stamping. Your fingerprints and facial image are taken on first entry, so don't worry if you don't find a stamp in your passport.
  • ETIAS: It applies only to visa-exempt travelers and does not apply to Schengen visa holders. It had not started operating as of the time of writing.

Common mistakes that lead to refusal

  • Booking dates that do not match the leave stated in the employer letter.
  • Depositing a large sum into the account a few days before applying with no clear source.
  • Choosing a consulate that does not actually represent the main destination.
  • A Turkish residence permit that expires only a very short time after the return.
  • Hiding a previous refusal or a previous visa.
  • Insurance that does not cover the whole length of the trip or has less coverage than required.

A suggested timeline for submitting your file

  1. Three months before travel: Decide on the main destination and the length of the trip, and check the validity of your passport and residence permit.
  2. Two and a half months before: Start monitoring available appointments at the application center, especially if you are traveling in summer or during the holidays.
  3. Two months before: Gather your bank statements, employer letter and social security record, and request sworn translations if needed.
  4. One week before the appointment: Prepare the flight and hotel reservations and the insurance, and review the whole file to make sure the dates are consistent.
  5. On the day of the appointment: Arrive early with the originals and copies in order, since any original document may be requested.

Tips for your bank statement

The bank statement is one of the most important parts of the file, because it proves that you can cover the costs of the trip and return to your life in Turkey. Make sure the statement reflects regular income and normal activity, not a sudden balance that appeared a few days before applying. If someone else is covering the costs of your trip, attach a sponsorship letter with their financial documents and proof of the family relationship. Some Schengen countries set daily reference amounts that differ from one country to another, but the consulate assesses the file as a whole: your job, income, trip length and previous travel history.

Beware of sellers of “guaranteed appointments”

The official application centers confirm that appointments are booked through their own websites, and they warn against parties that sell guaranteed appointments. At OYA we do not sell appointments or promise them. We help you prepare your file, fill in the application and follow the available booking opportunities through the official channels.

Questions & answers

Related questions

Can an Arab resident of Turkey apply for a Schengen visa from Istanbul?

Yes, a legal resident can apply at the consulate that covers their place of residence, with the Turkish residence permit. Many consulates require the residence permit to remain valid for a period after the return.

How much is the Schengen visa fee from Turkey?

The official fee is set by the European Union, and the application center fee is added to it. Fees may change, so we confirm them for you at the time of application. We submit the application on your behalf and put your file together correctly for a small service fee.

How long does a Schengen application take to process?

Usually 15 calendar days, and it may extend to 45 days in some cases. You can apply up to six months before travel.

Do you guarantee approval or an appointment?

No. The decision belongs to the consulate alone, and appointments are booked through official channels. Our role is to prepare an accurate, consistent file and follow it up.
