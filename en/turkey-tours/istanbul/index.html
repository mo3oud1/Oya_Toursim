Destination guide • Istanbul

Tourism in Istanbul: tours and programs for families

Tourism in Istanbul brings together Ottoman and Byzantine history, Bosphorus views and age-old bazaars in one city spanning two continents. In this guide you will find the top attractions, day trips and the areas to stay that suit families.

Why Istanbul?

Istanbul at a glance

Istanbul sits on both sides of the Bosphorus Strait, so half of it is in Europe and the other half in Asia, and it was the capital of both the Byzantine and Ottoman empires for many centuries. That is why in a single day you can find sultans' mosques, palaces overlooking the water, modern neighborhoods and large shopping centers. It is the first stop for most visitors to Turkey, and with a population of more than 15 million it is Turkey's largest and most vibrant city, and the starting point for destinations such as Bursa, Sapanca and Trabzon.

Most visitors start in the Sultanahmet area, where Hagia Sophia, the Blue Mosque and Topkapı Palace are steps from one another, then move on to a Bosphorus cruise, where they see Dolmabahçe Palace, the suspension bridges and the old wooden mansions on the shore. In the evening the Taksim area and İstiklal Avenue come alive, while Galata Tower offers one of the finest views over the Golden Horn. A visit is not complete without passing through the Grand Bazaar and the Spice Bazaar, or climbing Çamlıca Hill to see the whole city from the Asian side.

Istanbul suits families especially well: halal food is available almost everywhere, Arabic restaurants are common in Fatih, Aksaray and Taksim, and there are many options for children such as Miniatürk park, aquariums and large parks. Couples find a quiet, romantic atmosphere in dinner cruises and cafés on the Asian side. Mosques are found in almost every neighborhood, so it is easy to pray on time during tours and shopping.

Top sights

The most beautiful places to visit in Istanbul

A carefully chosen list of what is worth your time in Istanbul, with smart visit sequencing within our programs.

Hagia Sophia

An architectural masterpiece nearly 1,500 years old, built as a church in the Byzantine era and turned into a mosque after the Ottoman conquest, and prayers are held there today. Its enormous dome and decoration are among the most photographed in the city.

Sultanahmet Mosque (Blue Mosque)

It faces Hagia Sophia and is distinguished by its six minarets and the blue tiles that gave it its popular name. Entry is free, and it is closed to visitors during prayer times.

Topkapı Palace

The seat of the Ottoman sultans for about four centuries, with the Chamber of the Sacred Relics, the Treasury and gardens overlooking the meeting of the Bosphorus and the Sea of Marmara.

Bosphorus cruise

A boat ride between Europe and Asia passing Dolmabahçe Palace, Ortaköy Mosque and Rumeli Hisarı Fortress under the suspension bridges. Short daytime trips and evening dinner cruises are available.

Galata Tower

A 14th-century stone tower offering a circular view of the Golden Horn and the old city, and the alleys around it are full of cafés and craft shops.

Büyük Çamlıca Mosque

Turkey's largest mosque, opened in 2019 on Çamlıca Hill on the Asian side, with a wide view of the Bosphorus from its courtyard. An ideal place for Friday prayer or a quiet visit with the family.

Princes' Islands

An archipelago in the Sea of Marmara reached by ferry, and its best-known island is Büyükada. Private cars are not used there, so you get around by bicycle or electric carriage among the wooden houses and forests.

Grand Bazaar and Spice Bazaar

The Grand Bazaar (Kapalıçarşı) is one of the oldest and largest covered markets in the world, with thousands of shops for gold, carpets and gifts, and the nearby Spice Bazaar is the destination for spices, Turkish delight and nuts.

Taksim Square and İstiklal Avenue

The heart of modern Istanbul: a long pedestrian street with the historic red tram, lined with shops, restaurants and cafés, and lovely for a stroll after dinner.

Dolmabahçe Palace

A grand Ottoman palace on the Bosphorus shore, built in the 19th century, where the crystal chandeliers and the huge ceremonial hall will dazzle you. Arriving in the morning is best to avoid the queues.

Abu Ayyub al-Ansari Mosque and Pierre Loti Hill

The Eyüp district holds the tomb of the revered Companion Abu Ayyub al-Ansari (may God be pleased with him), and from there a cable car rises to the Pierre Loti café overlooking the Golden Horn.

Miniatürk

A miniature-model park on the shores of the Golden Horn displaying replicas of Turkey's most famous landmarks and former Ottoman lands, a fun and relaxing stop for children.

Day Trips

Tours and day trips from Istanbul

  • Old city tour: Hagia Sophia, the Blue Mosque, Topkapı Palace, the Basilica Cistern and the Süleymaniye Mosque, then shopping at the Grand Bazaar and the Spice Bazaar. It is best to start in the morning before the queues build up.
  • Bosphorus viewpoints tour by car: Dolmabahçe, Ortaköy, Bebek, Rumeli Hisarı Fortress and Emirgan Park, which glows with tulips in April, then crossing to the Asian side to visit Çamlıca Mosque and watch the sunset.
  • Day trip to Bursa and Mount Uludağ: the route over the Osmangazi Bridge or by ferry, then the Uludağ cable car, the Grand Mosque and the Silk Bazaar, and the return in the evening. Details in the Bursa tourism guide.
  • Day trip to Sapanca and Maşukiye: Lake Sapanca, the Maşukiye waterfalls and lunch on the riverbank, with optional activities such as ATV rides. Read more in the Sapanca guide.
  • Şile and Ağva: Ağva is a village between the river and the forest, and Şile has its castle and lighthouse on the Black Sea, plus lunch on the bank of the Göksu River. A lovely nature trip in summer and autumn, about two hours from the city.
  • Lake Abant and the Seven Lakes in Bolu: A long day trip to Lake Abant and Yedigöller (Seven Lakes) National Park, about three hours away, at its best in autumn colors or under winter snow.

Where to stay?

The best areas to stay in Istanbul

  • Sultanahmet (the Old City): Best for first-time visitors who want to walk to Hagia Sophia, the Blue Mosque and the Grand Bazaar. Hotels here are often smaller, and evenings are quieter.
  • Taksim and Şişli: The most popular choice with tourists: close to İstiklal Avenue, shopping centers and restaurants, and connected by metro. Good for families and for younger travelers who like to be where things happen.
  • Fatih, Aksaray and Laleli: An affordable area with many Arab restaurants and clothing shops, close to the tram to Sultanahmet. Suits mid-range budgets and families who prefer a conservative atmosphere.
  • The Bosphorus shore (Beşiktaş, Ortaköy and Bebek): Upscale hotels with direct water views and quiet waterfront cafés, ideal for honeymoons and for anyone looking for a luxury stay.
  • The Asian side (Üsküdar and Kadıköy): A calmer, more local feel with beautiful views of the European shore, close to Sabiha Gökçen Airport. Suits repeat visitors and those who prefer to stay away from the tourist crowds.

OYA tips

Tips before traveling to Istanbul

  • Both Istanbul Airport (IST) and Sabiha Gökçen (SAW) are about 40–50 km from Taksim, and the drive can take an hour or more at peak times, so we recommend booking your airport pickup in advance.
  • Plan your tours by area: one day for the Old City, one for the Bosphorus and one for the Asian side. Crossing between the two shores at rush hour takes a long time.
  • Modest dress is required when visiting mosques, women are asked to cover their heads, and shoes are removed. The interior halls are closed to visitors during prayer times.
  • The Istanbulkart covers the metro, tram and ferries, but a private car with an English-speaking driver is far more comfortable with children, older travelers and luggage.
  • Halal food is available almost everywhere, and Arab restaurants serving a variety of cuisines are common in Fatih, Aksaray and Taksim, so there is no need to worry about meals with the family.
  • Spring and autumn are the best seasons. Summer is crowded and hotel demand is high, while winter is rainy and cold but quieter, and often brings better hotel deals.

Questions & answers

Frequently asked questions about visiting Istanbul

How many days are enough to visit Istanbul?

4 to 5 days are enough to see the main sights: one day for the Old City, one for the Bosphorus, one for the Asian side and one for a trip out of town such as Bursa or Sapanca. With children or shopping lovers, we suggest 6–7 days at a relaxed pace. See our 5-day Istanbul itinerary.

What is the best area to stay in Istanbul for visitors?

Taksim and Şişli are the most popular for their closeness to shopping, restaurants and transport; Sultanahmet is ideal for history lovers; and Fatih and Aksaray are a budget option with many Arabic restaurants. For a honeymoon we recommend a hotel on the Bosphorus shore.

What are the main tourist attractions in Istanbul?

Hagia Sophia, the Blue Mosque, Topkapı Palace, a Bosphorus cruise, Galata Tower, Çamlıca Mosque, the Princes' Islands, the Grand Bazaar, İstiklal Avenue, Dolmabahçe Palace and the Abu Ayyub al-Ansari Mosque.

How much does a Bosphorus cruise cost?

The price varies a lot by type of trip: public ferries are the cheapest option, organized tourist cruises are slightly higher, and dinner cruises and private yachts are the most expensive. Because prices change by season, we send you the updated price on WhatsApp on request.

What are the best day trips from Istanbul?

The most popular are Bursa and Mount Uludağ, Sapanca and Maşukiye, Şile and Ağva, the Princes' Islands, and Lake Abant and the Seven Lakes. We arrange them with a private car and an English-speaking driver.

Is Istanbul suitable for children?

Yes, it has large gardens and parks, Miniatürk park, aquariums, amusement parks and boat trips that kids love. We only advise limiting the number of sights per day and avoiding travel at rush hour.

Which is better: Istanbul Airport or Sabiha Gökçen Airport?

Istanbul Airport (IST) is on the European side and is usually the better fit for staying in Taksim and Sultanahmet, while Sabiha Gökçen Airport (SAW) is on the Asian side and closer to Üsküdar and Kadıköy. We arrange pickup from both airports.
