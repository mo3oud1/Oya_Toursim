Istanbul hotels: what you should know

Looking for the right Istanbul hotels for your family? The first rule is that the area matters more than the number of stars. Istanbul is a huge city spread across two continents, and getting between its districts at rush hour can take an hour or more, so a good hotel near the metro or tram and close to the sights you plan to visit saves you more time and effort than a luxury hotel far from your plans. Being close to transport also lets you go back to rest in the middle of the day with the children and head out again in the evening, which makes a big difference on any family trip.

Most visitors choose among four main areas: Taksim and Şişli for shopping, activity and easy transport; Sultanahmet for historic sights on foot; Fatih and Aksaray for a conservative feel and Arabic restaurants on a mid-range budget; and the Bosphorus shores for views and quiet luxury. Each area has a type of stay that dominates: small hotels with historic character in the Old City, large hotels and serviced apartments in Taksim and Şişli, and upscale waterfront hotels in Beşiktaş and Ortaköy. On the Asian side, in Üsküdar and Kadıköy, there are quieter options for those who have visited before and prefer a local atmosphere away from the tourist crowds.

Large families often prefer serviced apartments because they combine several rooms, a living room and a kitchen in one unit, while a hotel with a Bosphorus view suits newlyweds for two special nights. Where to stay?

The best areas to stay in Istanbul

Choose the area according to your plans and travel style, and we will suggest suitable hotels within it.

Taksim and Şişli

Taksim & Şişli
Families and shopping

The heart of modern Istanbul: İstiklal Avenue, the big shopping malls, restaurants and metro stations. The choice of hotels and serviced apartments here is wide, across all categories. The downside is the crowds and evening noise, so ask for a room away from the main street if you prefer quiet.

FamiliesShopping

Sultanahmet (Old City)

Sultanahmet
First visit and historic sights

From here you can walk to Hagia Sophia, the Blue Mosque, Topkapı Palace and the Grand Bazaar, and some of its hotels have views of the Sea of Marmara or the minarets. Its hotels are usually smaller and the rooms tighter, and some are in restored old buildings. It suits couples and small families on a trip focused on history.

ViewsQuiet

Fatih, Aksaray and Laleli

Fatih, Aksaray & Laleli
Mid-range budget and a conservative feel

A lively area with many Arabic restaurants and clothing shops, close to the tram heading to Sultanahmet. Prices are usually lower than in Taksim and on the Bosphorus, and the atmosphere is closer to a conservative feel. Its streets are crowded by day and hotel standards vary, so choose carefully.

BudgetFamiliesShopping

Bosphorus shores: Beşiktaş, Ortaköy and Bebek

Beşiktaş, Ortaköy & Bebek
Honeymoon and luxury stays

Upscale hotels with direct views of the strait and the bridges, and quiet cafés on the water. They suit newlyweds and anyone who wants the luxurious side of Istanbul. Prices are in the higher range, and reaching the Old City takes a car or a ferry.

LuxuryViews

Asian side: Üsküdar and Kadıköy

Üsküdar & Kadıköy
Quiet and repeat visits

A calmer local atmosphere and lovely views of the European shore, with relative proximity to Sabiha Gökçen Airport. It suits those who have visited Istanbul before and want to avoid the tourist crowds. The downside is that most famous sights are on the other shore, so you will cross by ferry or metro every day.

Modern areas in the west of the city

Başakşehir & Beylikdüzü
Long stays and spacious apartments

Modern complexes and spacious serviced apartments near large shopping centers, chosen by some families for long stays. But they are far from the tourist sights, so we do not recommend them for a short trip unless you have a private car.

Types of stay

Where to stay in Istanbul

5-star hotels with a Bosphorus view

A view of the strait and the bridges and refined service, ideal for a honeymoon or two special nights at the end of the trip. A “partial view” is very different from a “full view”, so ask for confirmation of the room type.

Serviced apartments

Two or more bedrooms with a living room and kitchen, with reception and housekeeping as set by each property. The preferred choice for large families and for stays longer than a week.

Boutique hotels in the Old City

Small buildings with historic character, some with a rooftop breakfast restaurant overlooking the minarets or the sea. Rooms are smaller and some buildings have no elevator, so ask if you are traveling with older adults.

4-star hotels near the metro and tram

A good balance between price and location for those who spend the day sightseeing and come back only to sleep. What matters most is that the station is a few minutes' walk away.

Before you book

Check these details

  • The exact view type: Bosphorus, sea, city or interior
  • Is breakfast included, and for how many people per room?
  • Children's policy: free age, extra bed, and the maximum number of people per room
  • The actual walking distance to the nearest metro or tram station
  • Whether there is an elevator and parking, especially in Old City hotels
  • Check-in and check-out times, and whether early check-in is possible after a night flight
  • The cancellation and modification policy, and the last date for free cancellation

OYA tips

Booking tips

  • Book early for summer and for the Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha holidays, as the good options sell out first and prices rise as the date approaches. Winter is quieter and its deals are often better.
  • Choose the area according to your plans: Sultanahmet or Fatih if historic sights are your priority, and Taksim and Şişli if shopping and restaurants come first.
  • On a trip that combines Istanbul with Sapanca or Bursa, spend most nights in Istanbul and add one or two nights outside it instead of long day trips.
  • Both airports are about 40–50 km from Taksim, and the road can take over an hour at peak times, so book the airport pickup together with the hotel.
  • If you arrive at dawn, ask about early check-in, or book the night before so your room is ready when you arrive.

Questions & answers

Frequently asked questions about Istanbul hotels

What is the best area to stay in Istanbul for families?

It depends on your plans: Taksim and Şişli suit those who like shopping and easy transport, Fatih and Aksaray suit those who prefer a conservative feel and Arabic restaurants on a mid-range budget, and Sultanahmet suits those who want to walk to the historic sights. Send us the number of family members and the children's ages, and we will recommend the right area and type of stay.

Is it better to stay in Taksim or Sultanahmet?

Taksim is better for shopping, restaurants and nightlife, and it has more serviced apartments and spacious rooms. Sultanahmet is better for first-time visitors to Istanbul who want to see the sights on foot. On a trip of 5 days or more, you can split your stay between the two areas.

What is the difference between a hotel and an aparthotel?

A hotel offers a room with full services such as a restaurant, daily housekeeping and often breakfast, while a serviced apartment is a larger unit with bedrooms, a living room and a kitchen, with reception and services set by each property. An apartment suits large families and longer stays, and a hotel is more comfortable if you want full service.

Are there Bosphorus-view hotels suitable for families?

Yes, there are hotels on both shores of the Bosphorus with family rooms or connecting rooms, but they are in the higher price range and demand for them is high in summer. Our advice is to ask for written confirmation of the view type before booking, and to ask about connecting rooms if you have children.

Are there hotels near Istanbul Airport for transit?

Yes, there are places to stay inside the Istanbul Airport terminal and in the surrounding areas, suitable for a long layover or a very early flight. If you have a full night in the city, though, staying in the center with a private pickup lets you make better use of your time.
