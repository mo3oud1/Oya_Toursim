US visa from Turkey (B1/B2): the interview and documents

The US visa from Turkey (B1/B2 visitor visa) requires the DS-160 form and an in-person interview at the US Embassy in Ankara or the US Consulate General in Istanbul. At OYA Tourism we prepare your application and file, book your interview appointment and prepare you for it, with honesty and clarity from the start.

Overview

The steps begin with filling in the DS-160 form accurately and paying the application fee (MRV), then booking the interview appointment. Since September 2, 2025, an in-person interview has been required of almost all applicants, including children under 14 and older adults over 79, with limited exceptions.

Arab nationals residing in Turkey with legal residence can apply from Turkey, bringing their civil documents and proof of completed military service or exemption from it. Interview wait times vary a great deal and may stretch to several months in Istanbul, so we advise you to plan early and follow the official US Department of State wait-time tool.

We make a point of being upfront with you before you pay any fees: some nationalities are currently subject to US entry restrictions under a presidential proclamation in effect since January 1, 2026, which in most cases makes it impossible to issue visit visas to their citizens, including Syria, Yemen, Libya, Sudan and Somalia, and the restrictions also cover travel documents issued by the Palestinian Authority. These lists are reviewed periodically, so we check the status of your nationality first.

Our role includes filling in the DS-160, booking the appointment, preparing the file and coaching for the interview. The decision rests with the consular officer alone at the interview, and no one can guarantee it.

Who can apply?

  • Turkish citizens living in Turkey.
  • Arab nationals residing in Turkey with legal residence, such as Iraqis, Egyptians and Jordanians, with their civil documents.
  • Travelers for tourism, visiting family, medical treatment or short business trips such as conferences and meetings.
  • Citizens of countries covered by the entry restrictions: we explain the situation to you frankly before any step or fee.

Types and options

Types of US visa

B2: tourism and visits

For tourism, visiting family and friends, and medical treatment.

B1: business

For meetings, conferences and business negotiations, without working inside the United States.

Combined B1/B2

The most common form, covering both purposes. Its validity and number of entries vary by nationality, and the immigration officer determines the length of stay on arrival.

Checklist

Required papers and documents

The general list is below, and requirements may vary by nationality and country of residence — we confirm them for you before you apply.

  • A valid passport, and old passports that contain visas.
  • The DS-160 confirmation page and the fee payment receipt.
  • A photo that meets US specifications (5×5 cm).
  • Proof of employment and income: employer letter, payslips, SGK record or company documents.
  • Recent bank statements.
  • Proof of ties to Turkey: residence, work, family, property.
  • For non-Turkish residents: the Turkish residence permit and civil documents, and proof of completed military service or exemption from it.
  • An invitation letter or travel plan, if you have one.
The decision to grant a visa rests with the consular officer at the interview, and no agency can guarantee it.
The application fee is not refunded if the visa is refused.
Citizens of some countries, including Algeria and Tunisia, may be asked to pay a financial bond (Visa Bond) according to US Department of State lists; we check your situation before you apply.
An additional fee called the Visa Integrity Fee was enacted in 2025, and how it will be collected is not yet clear; we confirm the final amount with you at the time of application.
The entry restriction lists are reviewed periodically (about every 180 days), so we check the latest update before every application.
Provide honest and consistent information in the DS-160 and at the interview; any inconsistency may harm your current and future applications.

Questions & answers

US visa FAQ

Can an Arab national living in Turkey apply for a US visa from Istanbul?

Yes. The US Embassy in Turkey allows people residing legally to apply, and they must bring their civil documents and proof of military service or exemption from it. However, eligibility is affected by your nationality, and we check it first.

How long is the wait for a US visa interview appointment in Istanbul?

The wait varies a great deal and may stretch to several months, according to what the official US Department of State wait-time tool publishes. We monitor it for you and plan your application date according to your travel date.

Do children and older adults need an interview?

Since September 2, 2025, an in-person interview has been required of almost all applicants, including children under 14 and adults over 79, with limited exceptions.

I am Syrian or Yemeni. Can I get a US visa?

Under the US entry restrictions in effect since January 1, 2026, visit visas are mostly not issued to holders of passports from a number of countries, including Syria and Yemen. We know this is frustrating, which is why we are upfront with you before you pay any fees, and we can suggest suitable alternative destinations if you wish.

How much is the US visa fee?

The application fee (MRV) is set by the US government and is not refunded if the visa is refused. It may change, so we confirm it with you at the time of application. We help you fill in the DS-160 form and review it carefully, and you sign and submit it yourself. We also organize your file and prepare you for the interview for a small service fee that we explain to you before we start.

Do you guarantee approval of a US visa?

No, the decision rests with the consular officer alone. What we commit to is an accurate file, a DS-160 form free of errors and good preparation for the interview.
